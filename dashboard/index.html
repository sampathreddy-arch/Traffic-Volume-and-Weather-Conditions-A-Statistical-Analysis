🎲 Bayes' Theorem Laboratory (Module II)
Base Rate Fallacy Lab

Inverting observed highway congestion to find the posterior likelihood of adverse weather:
P(Rain | Congestion) = [P(Congestion | Rain) · P(Rain)] / P(Congestion)

Prior Probability of Rain: P(Rain) 5.06%
Likelihood: P(Congestion | Rain) 26.89%
Likelihood: P(Congestion | Dry) 24.92%
Posterior Probability P(Rain | High Congestion)
5.44%
Relative Risk Ratio: 1.08x

Even when traffic is at peak congestion, the probability that it is raining is merely 5.44%. Why? Because the prior base rate of rainfall is low (5.06%), and congestion occurs primarily due to scheduled commuter routines.

Academic Viva Defense & Examiner Critical Q&A

Strict Devil's Advocate Answers to Tough Evaluator Questions

Question 1: Causality vs Association
"Did you find that rainfall causes traffic volume reductions?"
No. In observational data, causal inference cannot be established without randomized assignment. Naive negative correlations between rain and traffic reflect seasonal and diurnal confounding. In our fully controlled regression model, the adjusted rain effect explains only ΔR² = 0.0009 (0.09%).
Question 2: Poisson Distribution Refutation
"Why didn't you fit a classical Poisson regression model?"
A Poisson variable requires equidispersion (Var = Mean). Our data has mean = 3,291.1 and variance = 3.94 × 10⁶, yielding a Dispersion Index D = 1,196.8 >> 1. The data is massively overdispersed with cyclical serial correlation, mathematically invalidating Poisson models.
Question 3: Autocorrelation & DW = 0.41
"How can you trust your OLS standard errors when Durbin-Watson is 0.412?"
We do not trust naive standard errors. Hourly traffic volume displays severe 24-hour diurnal serial correlation. We resolved this by deriving all hypothesis tests, p-values, and 95% confidence intervals using Newey-West HAC (Heteroscedasticity and Autocorrelation Consistent) standard errors with lag bandwidth = 24.
Question 4: Out-of-Sample Validation
"Why didn't you use random 5-fold cross-validation?"
Random cross-validation shuffles future data points into past training folds, causing fatal temporal data leakage in time-series data. We strictly enforced an honest chronological 80/20 train-test split (2012–2017 train vs 2017–2018 test).
